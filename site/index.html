Why this exists

Not another wrapper around an existing driver.

The project binds directly to QEMU’s Intel 8086:100e PCI function. It maps BAR0, programs the device, allocates coherent descriptor rings, maps packet buffers for DMA, and owns the complete network-device lifecycle.

The scope stays intentionally narrow—one RX queue, one TX queue, legacy descriptors, no offloads—so ownership and synchronization can be explained and defended line by line.

Recovery state machine

Reset without lying about synchronization.

Watchdog and RX allocation failures schedule process-context recovery. RTNL and a reset mutex serialize detach, IRQ synchronization, NAPI shutdown, DMA teardown, hardware reset, ring rebuild, and reattachment.

A real debugging story

The queue stopped.
The counter never moved.

Symptom

A deliberately stalled 64-entry TX ring stopped the network queue, but the ring-full transition was not reliably observable.

False lead

The first hypothesis blamed completion timing. The real problem was partly hidden by a ping-based reproducer that did not create a deterministic burst.

Root cause

Full state was detected only on a future transmit call—a call Linux was not required to make after honoring the queue stop.

Fix

Detect and act on the transition immediately after consuming the final usable descriptor, under the same TX lock used by cleanup.

The honest boundary

What this project does not claim.

  • × Physical silicon or board-level bring-up
  • × Production-driver or upstream parity
  • × ENA, RDMA, or physical-PMD performance
  • × KASAN, lockdep, or executed MSI coverage

Those are next experiments, not retroactive résumé language. The current result is a QEMU-validated Linux driver and a functional DPDK virtual-PMD path.
